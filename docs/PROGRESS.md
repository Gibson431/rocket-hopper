The step file left both open. `x0` is copied, never mutated.
- 2026-10-17, S02: `integrate` calls `callback(t, x)` after every step with the post-step time, snapped to exactly `t1` on the final (possibly shortened) step. Step times are `t0 + k*dt`, not accumulated, to avoid drift. A zero-length interval returns `x0` with no steps; `dt <= 0` raises ValueError.
- 2026-10-17, S02: `rigid_body_derivative` keeps the specified `force_I`/`torque_B` argument names with `# noqa: N803`; ruff's pep8-naming would otherwise reject the frame-suffix convention.

The step's "moves monotonically" test is therefore applied to the late burn only (<= 13 kg), plus a test pinning the early dip.
- 2026-10-17, S03: gimbal limits. The angle derivative uses the rate clamped to +/- `gimbal_rate_max` (so angle changes obey the rate limit exactly) and is zero at the angle limit when moving outward; the thrust direction clips the angle to +/- `gimbal_max`. The raw angle state may overshoot the limit by at most one step of rate*dt under RK4. The rate state itself is not clamped, only its outward acceleration is zeroed at the limit.

- 2026-10-19, S10: the IMU reaches the filter at the controller rate (the engine keeps the newest of the four 400 Hz samples per call), so each call is one IMU measurement with the per-sample noise (`density * sqrt(rate)`), integrated with the trapezoid rule over the call interval. - 2026-10-19, S10: `EkfParams` (in `ekf.py`) carries everything the filter knows, because `hopper.estimation` may not import the config; `scenario_glue.ekf_params_from_config` builds it (measurement sigmas floored at 1e-4 so noise-free sensors do not make S singular). `EstimatedController` lives in `hopper/estimated_controller.py`, outside both packages, since control and estimation may not import each other. `run_hop(..., perfect_state=False, ekf_params=None)` and `run_scenario.py --mission hop --ekf` use it.
- 2026-10-19, S10: latency handling is the buffered replay (preferred option). Frames hold the post-propagation state, raw IMU inputs and the measurements applied there; a late sample is attached to the nearest frame and the history is re-propagated. The filter's time axis is the IMU measurement time, `imu_delay = latency + 0.5 / rate` (3.25 ms) before the call; a sample taken at `s` is applied at `s + imu_delay`. Sub-frame offsets are handled by moving position/height forward by `v * dt` in the measurement model. History is 0.5 s; older samples are dropped and counted (`n_stale`). The estimate is not extrapolated over `imu_delay`.
- 2026-10-19, S10: lidar is used as a range measurement `p_z / R33` (with the attitude Jacobian), not as a pre-converted altitude `range * cos(tilt)`. It is the same geometry, but keeps the noise model on the raw range and the attitude correlation. Gated while estimated tilt < 45 deg.
- 2026-10-19, S10: alignment averages 1.0 s of static data (roll/pitch from the accelerometer, yaw = 0, position from GPS mean and lidar/baro, velocity 0). The gyro bias is NOT initialised from the static gyro mean (the prior from config is more precise, and the bias test must learn it from motion). The mission starts at the first estimate, so take-off is ~0.5 s later than with perfect state.
- 2026-10-19, S10: innovation gates are NIS thresholds (baro, lidar, ZUPT 16; GPS 6-dof 30). After `reject_timeout` = 1 s of continuous rejections a sensor is trusted again and the observed states are re-seeded (covariance + 10 R for that update). Without it a collapsed P (noise-free sensors, or a post-impact error) rejects a sensor forever. A single 50 m spike is still rejected.
- 2026-10-19, S10: propellant mass is dead-reckoned in `estimation/mass.py` from the commanded throttle through a first-order lag (throttle_tau), clamped to the throttleable range like the engine. Error over a full hop: < 1e-4 kg (command delay not modelled).

- 2026-10-19, S10: the contact impact at touchdown is a few ms long and the 100 Hz IMU cannot resolve it: the noise-free estimate is accurate to mm in flight but picks up ~0.08 m/s velocity error at touchdown (corrected by GPS/lidar). The noise-free tracking test therefore covers the flight up to first touchdown.
